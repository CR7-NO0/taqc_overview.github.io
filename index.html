Autonomous UUV Control for Multi-Waypoint Tracking through Distributional Reinforcement Learning

TAQC: Truncated Adaptive Quantile Critics

An end-to-end, map-free UUV multi-waypoint controller combining adaptive distributional reinforcement learning with domain randomization for robust tracking in uncertain underwater environments.

Main contributions

  • End-to-end UUV tracking control：RL framework for sequential waypoint tracking.
  • Distributional RL：Return-distribution modeling improves learning efficiency and stability.
  • TAQC method：TQC extended with a learnable fraction proposal network and a mass-weighted critic loss.
  • Domain randomization：Enhance the generalization capability for unseen trajectories under disturbances.

Research motivation

Traditional UUV path-following methods have shown good performance in various conditions. However, complex underwater environments and changing task requirements call for more adaptive control methods. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a promising approach by learning control policies through interactions with the environment.

Conventional reinforcement learning methods, such as PPO and SAC, mainly rely on expected returns for value estimation without explicitly modeling the full return distribution, potentially overlooking return variability. In addition, some existing distributional reinforcement learning methods use fixed quantile locations, limiting their ability to represent complex return distributions. Therefore, we explore a more flexible approach to return distribution modeling to potentially improve learning efficiency and stability.

Reinforcement learning policies may overfit to training conditions, leading to performance degradation in unseen environments or tasks. Therefore, we adopt domain randomization to improve policy adaptability to environmental changes and enhance generalization across different conditions.

1

End-to-end control

End-to-end reinforcement learning enables UUVs to learn control policies directly from state observations to control commands, reducing the need for accurate hydrodynamic models and simplifying controller design. This approach also allows UUVs to learn coordinated control actions through interaction with the environment, offering a flexible solution for path following under uncertain underwater conditions.

2

Adaptive quantile representation

An FPN generates non-uniform quantile fractions for flexible return-distribution modeling.

3

Truncated multi-critic learning

A pooled multi-critic target with truncation is used to reduce overestimation and improve stability.

4

Domain randomization

Training-time randomization of disturbances, parameters, and initial states improves robustness and generalization.

Algorithmic framework

Building upon TQC, TAQC explicitly models the return distribution within a distributional reinforcement learning framework. Its main components include a learnable Fraction Proposal Network (FPN), multiple quantile critics, a truncation mechanism for the upper tail of the mixture distribution, and domain randomization throughout training.

  • End-to-end control：End-to-end reinforcement learning directly learns the mapping from state observations to control commands through environmental interactions, reducing reliance on accurate dynamic models and simplifying controller design.
  • Adaptive fraction proposal：The FPN generates non-uniform quantile fractions conditioned on each state-action pair.
  • Multi-critic truncation：Quantile atoms from target critics are pooled into a mixture and the right tail is truncated to reduce overestimation.
  • Mass-weighted critic loss：The critic objective is reconstructed so that each quantile term is weighted by its interval probability mass.
  • Domain randomization：Ocean disturbances, hydrodynamic parameters, and initial states are randomized during training.

Task description

The UUV starts from a randomized initial region and tracks a sequence of waypoints under disturbances. When the 3D distance to the current waypoint falls below a threshold, the target switches to the next waypoint. The problem is formulated as a continuous-state, continuous-action reinforcement-learning task.

Baselines and experimental setting

All experiments are conducted in the bluerov2_gym environment. The 2D training task uses a circle of radius 5 m; the 3D training task uses a helix of the same radius with 30 waypoints. Each episode lasts at most 800 steps. The training budgets are 500k steps for the 2D task and 1M steps for the 3D task.

Classical control

  • FAPID：Fuzzy Adaptive PID as a non-learning controller baseline.
  • LADRC：Linear Active Disturbance Rejection Control.

Expectation-based RL

  • PPO：A representative on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm.
  • SAC：A representative off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm.

Distributional RL baselines

  • TQC：Truncated Quantile Critics with quantile-distribution learning and truncation, used to evaluate the benefits of adaptive quantile modeling.
  • TIQC：A comparison algorithm developed in this study by integrating an implicit quantile network into TQC.
  • DSAC-T：Distributional Soft Actor-Critic with Three Refinements. A recently proposed distributional reinforcement learning algorithm.
  • TAQC (ours)：TQC extended with a learnable fraction proposal mechanism and mass-weighted critic loss.

Evaluation metrics

  • ATE (Average 3D Tracking Error): Mean 3D distance between the agent and the current target waypoint.
  • ARE (Average Radial Error): Average XY-plane radial error.
  • AZE (Average Z-Axis Error): Average vertical tracking error.
  • AHE (Average Heading Error): Average angular difference between actual and desired heading.
  • SR (Success Rate): Proportion of successful episodes, mainly used in generalization and robustness experiments.
  • Steps (Episode / Training Steps): Evaluation episodes report completion steps; training additionally reports first-success and convergence steps.

Experimental results and analysis

Simulation results for 2D tracking, 3D tracking, unseen trajectories, and disturbance robustness

2D path-following experiments

In the 2D circular tracking task, TAQC and TQC reliably solve the task. TAQC converges more efficiently, with lower variance and fewer task-completion steps. DSAC-T occasionally reaches positive rewards without a reliably converged policy, while TIQC, SAC, and PPO fail to learn a consistently successful policy under the tested disturbances. TAQC achieves competitive tracking accuracy while requiring fewer steps to complete the task than TQC.

3D path-following experiments

For 3D helical waypoint tracking, TAQC and TQC converge to positive returns, with TAQC generally learning faster and more consistently. TIQC exhibits greater variability, DSAC-T learns more slowly, and SAC/PPO do not converge satisfactorily. The tabulated comparison shows TAQC has the lowest ATE and ARE and the fewest evaluation steps among the methods able to reliably complete the task.

3D generalization experiments

The learned 3D policy is evaluated on six previously unseen paths: S-shape, double helix, polyline, figure-eight, conical helix, and comb scanning. TAQC succeeds on all six path families at high rates, whereas several distributional RL baselines encounter low or zero success rates on specific trajectories.

  • TAQC success rates on the six trajectories are 100%, 100%, 100%, 100%, 100%, and 98%, respectively.
  • TQC, TIQC, and DSAC-T show failures or substantially reduced success rates on some unseen trajectories.

Robustness with domain randomization

On the challenging comb-scanning task, the paper compares TAQC trained with and without domain randomization over five increasingly difficult disturbance settings. With domain randomization, the controller generally maintains higher success rates and lower tracking errors as disturbances intensify.
